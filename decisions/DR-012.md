## What the migration run establishes

**The mechanism is not what any of our recent framings said.** Three facts, all from pre-registered, gated runs:

1. **The wedge tracks CPU *identity*, not migration or the affinity mask.** Pinned to CPU 10: 0 of 8 stalls. Pinned to CPU 46: 5 of 8. Unpinned: 8 of 8. Both 10 and 46 are off the IRQ's mask, yet one is safe and one isn't. So "off-mask" is not the predictor, and the frozen "pinning fixes it" prediction (0 of 8 on CPU 46) was **falsified**. This is the sharpest new fact, and it kills the simple placement story.

2. **The core scheduler wake path is exonerated.** Of 233 classified stranded gaps, 0 were wake-lost (a wake issued, the thread never ran). 99 were event-silent (no wake even attempted) and 35 wake-flowing. Event-silent dominating points at the **driver or device**, not the kernel scheduler. That's a clean negative result that removes a whole candidate.

3. **Migration moments are enriched about 14×.** In the forced-hop arm, 27% of stranded gaps began within 2 ms of a CPU change, against a 2% chance rate. So the *act* of moving does matter, but the gap then persists while the thread sits on CPU 46 (63% of samples). Migration triggers; residence sustains.

## What the source says, and the hypothesis it yields

I read the mlx5 arming code at v6.17.8. There's a concrete candidate for the event-silent gaps:
- Arming a completion queue writes a doorbell stamped with `arm_sn & 3` — a 2-bit sequence number.
- The **completion-event interrupt handler increments `arm_sn`** (`++cq->arm_sn` in `mlx5_eq_comp_int`).

That is a classic arm-sequence handshake: an arm only stays valid if its sequence number matches what the device expects. If a poll on one CPU and an event or arm on another interleave so that `arm_sn` advances between reading it and writing the doorbell, the arm is stale and **no interrupt is delivered for completions already sitting in the queue** — exactly the event-silent, ready-but-unserved signature the probe sees. The `mlx5_add_cq_to_tasklet` comment even warns that completions can still arrive on old EQs during CQ migration.

So the testable hypothesis is: **the event-silent stalls are a stale-arm race on `arm_sn`, made reachable when the poll runs on a different CPU (and EQ) than the one servicing the queue's events.** That would explain all three facts at once: migration triggers the interleaving, CPU identity matters because it selects which EQ/IRQ path is involved, and the scheduler wake path is irrelevant because the failure is in device arming, not thread wakeup.

This is a hypothesis, not a finding. But it's specific, it's grounded in the v6.17.8 source, and it's falsifiable.

If the stale-arm race confirms, that is a **more** interesting result than the contract framing, and it fits the metastability lens cleanly: migration is the trigger, the stale arm is the sustaining effect (no event will ever re-arm until traffic happens to generate one), and CPU locality sets the hidden capacity.
