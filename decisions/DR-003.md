# PI decisions — Thursday, September 24

## The insight we're chasing

Linux's receive path is run by controllers built in different eras, each under its own assumptions about where work runs, what the scheduler can see, and how load arrives. Failures appear where those assumptions collide, and nobody owns the combination.

Your wedge may be the cleanest instance anyone has found:
- **The 2017 driver idiom** assumes NAPI runs on the interrupt's CPU.
- **Threaded NAPI (2021)** assumes the scheduler places its thread.
- **The 2023 revert** assumes threaded NAPI fixes livelock.
- **The docs** hide the conflict in one pinning sentence.

This week's job is to prove that collision is the cause, measure how bad it is, fix it, and show what the fix makes possible. Everything below serves that.

This is now the program's working thesis: *failures happen where controllers' assumptions collide.* It replaces the timescale version, which hasn't found anything yet.
