## Decision needed
Fig. 1's claim reads "Inline collapses past the knee; deferral caps near
half a core; separation drains more." The complete dataset partially
falsifies it and partially strengthens it:

(goodput share of offered, n=2-3, bootstrap CIs in
checkpoints/2026-09-24-fig1-3-dataset-complete.md)

- HOLDS and sharpens: separation drains more -- but the separation that
  drains is separating the APPLICATION from the interrupt core while
  processing stays inline (P0X: 74-98% under flood).
- FAILS as stated: the threaded placements (the ladder's mechanism) do
  not drain at flood; they wedge (C-008: 24/24 cells, onset t=2..68).

## Proposed resolution (option A; alternatives below)
Restrict the claim to what the data shows: "separating network
processing from the application drains more -- when the separation
keeps the queue's processing inline and moves the application (or,
sub-flood, threads it). Under sustained flood the threaded placements
are wedge-limited (C-008)." Consequences: Fig. 1 carries P0X as the top
line and wedge markers; the ladder (Fig. 5) gains a wedge re-arm story
and its rung-2 semantics are revisited (p1ctl v2: on wedge detection,
fall back to inline-with-app-separated or re-thread); the intro's
separation sentence follows.

Option B: keep the claim and mark Fig. 1 Dropped (rejects the paper's
own strongest measurement; not recommended).
Option C: hold the claim list until the Fig. 4 cells + a P0X-vs-threaded
flood A/B (5 reps) confirm or refute the table (recommended sequencing:
this DR closes after those cells).
