# CHECKPOINT — H1/H2 contract verdict (interim): FAILED
Written: 2026-09-24 01:35 UTC · Experiment davidlin-317389 · repo cloudlab-profile (main)

## Status: the clock contract does not hold in this realization

Plan-of-record contract ("LLM Serving Runs on a Clock"): "plateau +
drops track packet rate or alignment -> H1/H2 hold." Neither condition
is met:

- No plateau: after the timer fix (AN-001), the D matrix delivers 100%
  of demand in every mode at 25k-75k (1.00M / 2.00M / 3.00M tok/s),
  and ~100% at 100k in random/per-engine. The old 1.5M tok/s
  "plateau" and the ~1.85M "endpoint ceiling" were both the emitter's
  silently dropped ticks — superseded by AN-001.
- Drops do not track packet rate or alignment: softnet dropped
  0.002%, squeeze 0, retrans +6k total over the whole A pass at 2M+
  pps; emitter gates clean (drops ~0) across the D matrix.

H1 (packet-rate overload in softirq): UNSUPPORTED. The kernel was
never the limiter at these scales; the ceilings were user-space (the
emitter timer first; HAProxy's 13.2-core per-token tax is the next
candidate ceiling above the endpoint one).

H2 (step-synchronized bursts -> the full story): FAILS the contract,
but the verdict must carry its covariate: THE TREATMENT NEVER FORMED
AT THE WIRE. burst_analyze on the proxy-NIC captures: "aligned"
emission at 50k and 150k puts p50 0.040-0.042 of each step's packets
in the step's first 1ms — identical to the random-phase baseline
(0.040). One-token-one-segment at these rates serializes the step
across ~the whole period (25k write()s per 1ms slot); achieved wire
alignment is ~uniform in every cell. So the aligned-vs-random null in
the D matrix is null BY CONSTRUCTION. What is refuted is the
one-token-one-segment burst model, not the clock mechanism itself.

What survives the refutation (measured, stands): the wire carries a
25ms sawtooth arrival process — phase-locked periodic rate modulation,
visible even through the smear. The "smooth independent arrivals"
assumption of the host is still violated, as rate waves rather than
instant incast. Any write-up states it exactly that way.

## H3 / H4 status
- H3 (TCP memory pressure): not supported either — no sockstat

- H4 (proxy limit): alive and now the leading candidate. HAProxy
  measured at 1,324% CPU (13.2 cores) for 1.88M tok/s relay
  (worker threads ~85% each) with quiet kernel counters — a per-token
  user-space tax at the proxy. Per the plan's hypothesis table, H4
  is the fallback to the Reins host paper (NSDI '28).

## The one test still owed to H2 (recommendation, PI decision)
The blueprint's fix set says it plainly and the smear confirms it:
coalesce-by-step + multiplex (fixes #1-2) are not optimizations, they
are the ENABLER of a wire burst at all. H2 has not had a fair test —
no run so far produced the burst whose effect it predicts. Decision
point: (a) build the coalesce+multiplex emitter (one connection, all
streams' frames, one write per step) and re-run the phase contrast;
H2 lives or dies on THAT data. (b) If H2 still fails with a real
burst, fall to H4 -> Reins host paper per the kill logic. Recommend
(a) first: the current refutation is of a rig artifact class, and (a)
is also the paper's fix #1/#2 ablation either way.
